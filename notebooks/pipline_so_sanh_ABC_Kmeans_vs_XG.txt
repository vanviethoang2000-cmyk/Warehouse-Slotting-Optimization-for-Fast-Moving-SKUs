1. Stage 1: Future Forecasting (Inference)
Input: Test Data containing features of November and December 2010 (including lag variables, seasonality 'month', event 'is_promo'...).
Process: Load data into the winning Machine Learning model from Stage 3 (e.g., Multiple Linear Regression or XGBoost).
Output: Forecasted sales quantity column (pred_quantity) for each SKU for each day of November and December.

2. Stage 2: Ranking & Indexing
Input: Forecast results table from Stage 1.
Process: 
1. Groupby total forecasted sales quantity for each SKU over both months.
2. Sort the SKU list by potential from high to low.
3. Split the list into 10 equal parts (qcut).
Output: Each SKU possesses a Velocity Index from 1 to 10. (Convention: Index 1 is the "Star" best-seller, Index 10 is the slowest-moving inventory).

3. Stage 3: Dynamic Slotting Simulation
Input: SKU list with Velocity Index and a Network of shelf coordinates (Grid Slots) measured by distance to the warehouse door (I/O Point).
Process: The algorithm applies the "Priority by Index" rule. SKUs with Index 1 will automatically "occupy" the shelf positions closest to the I/O Point. SKUs with higher Indexes will be gradually pushed back to further areas (Zone B, Zone C).
Output: A complete physical Warehouse Layout, actively arranged to "anticipate" the demand in November-December.

4. Stage 4: Execution Simulation and Evaluation
Input: AI warehouse layout from Stage 3 and ACTUAL sales data (actual_quantity) of November-December.
Process: 
* Outbound order simulation system: Staff pick items exactly according to what customers actually bought in November-December.
Formula: Total distance = Actual pick frequency * Shelf distance * 2 (round trip).
Output: KPI "Total travel distance (m)". This figure is plotted on a Bar Chart to compete directly with the results of the static ABC and K-Means strategies from Stage 2.

This pipeline creates a perfect loop: Taking past data to predict the future, and taking actual future data to prove the validity of the prediction.
